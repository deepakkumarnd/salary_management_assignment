# Salary Management — Requirements (v1)

## Goal
Replace ACME's spreadsheet-based salary tracking with a web application where **HR Managers** sign in to their
**organization** and can
(1) set up the organization's defaults (countries it operates in, allowances, departments, job roles),
(2) define how each role is paid (basic pay, allowances, variable pay, overtime rate) per country,
(3) maintain accurate salary records for ~10,000 employees across multiple countries, **including every salary revision
with its effective date and when someone's employment ends**,
(4) record **deductions** for a month (unpaid leave, other deductions) and hand each month's pay to payroll as a
**CSV export with the monthly amounts for that month** (prorated, with deductions), and
(5) answer "how do we pay people?" questions in seconds instead of building pivot tables.

**Success looks like:** the HR Manager can set up a role's pay structure once, find any employee, record a raise
effective from any date, record a leaver, download a country's payroll file for a month, and answer "what is the
min / max / average / median pay in country X" and "what does job role Y earn in country X" without leaving the app,
with pages responding in well under a second at 10k records.

## Persona & key questions
*HR Manager*: one of several HR users in the organization, comfortable with Excel, not technical. Typical questions:
- What is the pay range and average in **India** (or any country), on basic, on gross (fixed) and on total target pay including variable pay?
- What does a **Software Engineer** earn in **Germany**, and how does that compare to other roles there?
- If I raise HRA across India, or a special allowance for one role, what does pay look like afterwards?
- How many people do we employ per country and department?
- When did **Priya** last get a raise, by how much, and why?
- For **September payroll in India**: who is on it, who joined or left, whose pay changed (including backdated changes), what each person's pay is for September after unpaid leave and other deductions, and the totals?

## In scope (v1)
| Area | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Sign-in** | Email + password login for **HR users only**. **Employees cannot sign in**: an employee is a record that HR manages, with no account, no password and no view of the app. There is **no public sign-up**: the seed script creates the first HR user, and HR users add colleagues (below). Includes forgot/reset password, lock-out after 5 failed attempts, and sign-out after 30 minutes of inactivity. There is **no "remember me"**: every session ends after 30 minutes of inactivity, because this is salary data. |
| **Organization** | **Everything belongs to an organization**: employees, departments, job roles, allowances, pay structures, insights, exports. Each HR user belongs to exactly one organization and can only ever see and change that organization's data. |
| **Organization setup** | The **first thing after login**, until it is completed: a short guided setup of the organization's defaults. (1) Organization name, **employee ID prefix** (e.g. `ACME`), and standard working hours per week (used for the overtime hourly rate, default 40). (2) Countries the organization operates in, chosen from the supported list. (3) Country allowances for those countries (optional, e.g. HRA 40% in India). (4) Departments and job roles, starting from an editable suggested list. The rest of the app is unavailable until setup is completed. Afterwards, the same defaults can be edited from Settings. |
| **Team** | HR users can add colleagues to their organization by name and email. The new user gets an email link to set their own password. HR users can remove colleagues, but not themselves. Removing a colleague **deactivates** their account: they can no longer sign in, they disappear from Team, and their name stays on the changes they made. A deactivated account's **email becomes free again**, so the same person can be given a new account later, e.g. when they rejoin, including in another organization (such as another country's entity). Re-adding someone to the same organization reactivates their old account. |
| **Country allowances** | Per **country**: named allowances, each set as a **% of basic** (e.g. HRA 40% in India). They apply to **every employee in that country**, whatever their role, so a country-wide policy is set, and changed, in one place. Countries with no such policy (e.g. Germany) simply have none. |
| **Pay structures** | Per **country + job role**: a default annual basic pay, **role-specific allowances** as a % of basic *on top of* the country's (e.g. Special Allowance 15% for Software Engineers), a **target variable pay as a % of gross (fixed) pay** (e.g. 10% for engineers, 40% for sales; 0% means no variable pay), and an **overtime rate as a % of basic hourly pay** (e.g. 150%). Create, view, edit. A structure can be deleted only if no employee, current or former, has ever used it. |
| **Employee records** | Create, view and edit employees: full name, email, job role, department, country, employment type, hire date. Each employee gets a **readable employee ID** such as `ACME-00042`. It is generated automatically in sequence per organization, never changes, is never reused, and is used everywhere HR refers to a person, including page URLs. Internal database IDs are never shown. Each employee is linked to the pay structure for their country + role. **Employees are never deleted**; people who leave are recorded as leavers (below). **Country cannot be changed after creation**: a move to another country is recorded as a leaver (reason *internal transfer*) plus a new employee in the new country, because pay, currency and payroll are all per country. |
| **Salary revisions** | Every change to an employee's basic pay is a **salary revision** with an **effective date**, a **reason** (hire, appraisal, promotion, market adjustment, correction) and optional notes. The hire is the first revision. Revisions can be **backdated** (e.g. entered on 20 Sep, effective 1 Apr) or **scheduled** for a future date; a scheduled one takes effect automatically on its date. The employee page shows the full history: date, new basic, change vs. previous (amount and %), reason, and who entered it. Revisions are never edited or deleted. A wrong one is **voided** with a reason (it stays visible, struck through) and replaced by a correct one. |
| **Employee pay** | An employee's **current basic pay** is the latest revision in effect today. New hires' basic is **pre-filled from the role's default basic**, and HR can adjust it. Changing a role's default basic only affects *new* hires and never silently changes anyone's pay. Allowances and **gross pay** (basic + allowances) are always *calculated* from the employee's basic and the percentages of their **country's allowances plus their role's allowances**. Changing an allowance % therefore updates everyone it applies to immediately. The employee view shows the breakdown: basic, each allowance (labelled country or role), gross, target variable pay, **total target pay** (gross + target variable), and overtime rate. HR decides allowances and variable pay % by policy (country, role), not per person. Variable pay is a *target* (what the employee earns at 100% performance), not a record of what was actually paid. |
| **End of employment** | HR records a leaver with their **last working day**, a **reason** (resignation, termination, retirement, end of contract, internal transfer, recorded in error, other) and optional notes. Status comes from the dates: **Active** (no end date), **Leaving** (end date in the future, e.g. serving notice), **Exited** (end date passed). Exited employees are read-only, and their history stays available forever. An exit can be **undone** (e.g. a resignation is withdrawn). A former employee who is rehired gets a **new record and a new employee ID**. *Recorded in error* is how a record created by mistake is retired: those records are left out of insights and payroll exports. |
| **Currency** | All amounts are whole numbers in the **local currency of the country**: pay is defined annually, and the payroll export shows the monthly amounts. Currency comes from the country, so it can never be mismatched. |
| **Finding people** | Paginated employee list (server-side) with: **one search box** that matches **name, email or employee ID** (case-insensitive, partial match, e.g. `priya`, `@acme.com`, `00042`); **filters by country, department and job role**, plus a **status filter** (default *Active + Leaving*; also *Active*, *Leaving*, *Exited*, *All*), usable individually or in any combination, which **combine with the search** (all conditions must match); sortable columns (gross pay is shown but not sortable); and a result count ("1–25 of 1,243"). Search and filter state is kept in the URL, so a filtered list can be bookmarked or shared, and Back works. Scales to 10k+ rows. |
| **Monthly deductions** | For a given employee and month, HR records **unpaid leave** (number of days, in half days) and **other deductions** (amount + description, e.g. "salary advance recovery", "notice-period shortfall"). Several other deductions per month are allowed. Unpaid leave is valued at the employee's daily gross rate for that month. Deductions can be entered on the employee page, or on a per-country, per-month **Deductions** page for entering many at once. They can be added for a leaver's final months even after they have exited. Deductions are editable (they are monthly inputs, not history). |
| **Payroll export (CSV)** | Per **country** and **pay month**, download a CSV of **everyone employed at any point in that month**, one row per employee, with five columns: **employee ID**, **employee name**, **gross salary** for the month (basic + allowances, prorated by calendar days for joiners, leavers and mid-month salary revisions), **deductions** (unpaid leave + other deductions) and **net salary** = gross − deductions (before tax). Before downloading, HR sees a summary: headcount, joiners, leavers, pay changes, total gross, deductions and net, plus warnings such as a negative net salary or data changed since the month was last exported. Every export is logged (who, when, country, month, rows, checksum). The file opens cleanly in Excel. |
| **Validation** | Required fields, valid email that is unique among current employees, positive basic pay, allowance, variable pay and overtime percentages between 0 and a sane upper bound, allowance names unique across a country and its roles (a role cannot redefine a country allowance, and a country allowance cannot be added while a role in that country already uses the name), hire date not in the future, country among the organization's operating countries, and an existing pay structure for the employee's country + role. Revisions: effective date on or after the hire date and not after the last working day, and at most one revision per employee per date. Leavers: last working day on or after the hire date, and no scheduled revisions after it. Deductions: only for a month in which the person was employed; unpaid leave in half-day steps, at most the days employed that month, and one unpaid-leave entry per employee per month; other deductions a positive amount with a description. HR accounts: email unique among *active* accounts. Clear, field-level error messages. |
| **Insights: by country** | For every country: headcount of **current employees** (Active + Leaving), currency, and min / max / average / median of **basic**, **gross** and **total target** pay, all as of today. |
| **Insights: drill-down** | For a selected country: the same metrics broken down by job role and by department. |
| **Concurrent edits** | Several HR Managers may use the app, but edits are infrequent, so **optimistic locking** is enough. Every employee, pay structure and country allowance carries a version number and records who last changed it. An update must send the version it was based on. If someone else saved in the meantime, the change is rejected (HTTP 409) and the UI tells the user the record was changed (and by whom), shows the latest data, and lets them re-apply their edit. Two revisions for the same employee and date, or voiding a revision twice, are rejected the same way. Nothing is silently overwritten. |
| **Seed data** | The seed script creates the **ACME organization** and its **first HR user**, and prints the login. Setup is left pending, so the first login goes through organization setup. A separate demo task fills a set-up organization with pay structures and **10,000 realistic employees** with several years of salary revisions, leavers and a few scheduled raises, so every feature can be demonstrated at scale. |

## Deliberately out of scope (and why)
| Left out | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **Employee self-service** | Employees viewing their own pay, history or payslips would need a separate, much more restricted access model (each person sees only their own record) and extra safeguards for salary data. The stated user is HR, so employees have no access in v1. |
| **SSO, 2FA, self sign-up, roles & permissions** | Email + password covers v1. SSO through the company IdP and two-factor auth are the next hardening steps for salary data. There is no public sign-up because organizations are provisioned, not self-served. All HR users have the same permissions: finer roles (e.g. read-only, per-country access) need a permissions model nobody has asked for yet. |
| **Multiple organizations per user, org switching, cross-org reporting** | One user belongs to one organization, which keeps data isolation simple and easy to verify. |
| **Arrears** | A salary revision backdated to an earlier month changes pay only from the month it is first included in; earlier months aren't recalculated, so no arrears are paid or recovered through the payroll file. HR settles such differences with the payroll provider. Arrears can be added later: revisions keep their effective date, entry time and void time, and every export is logged. |
| **Tax, statutory deductions and contributions, payslips, bank files, filings** | Income tax, social security, PF/ESI, NI and similar deductions are regulated, change every year and differ in each country. That is payroll processing, usually run by a provider per country. v1 computes pay **up to net before tax** (monthly gross, unpaid leave, other deductions), and the provider takes it from there. |
| **Leave management, attendance, additions** | Unpaid-leave *days* are entered by HR per month. Leave balances, leave requests and attendance tracking belong to a leave/time system. One-off additions (bonuses, reimbursements) are not in v1. |
| **Direct payroll-provider integrations (APIs, SFTP)** | Each provider and country has its own format and contract. A standard CSV works with all of them today, and integrations can be added per provider later. |
| **Dated history of pay-policy changes (allowance %, variable %, overtime %) and of role/department changes** | v1 dates **basic pay** (salary revisions) and **employment** (hire/end), which covers the most frequent and most disputed changes. Policy percentages and role/department apply as they are today. **Consequence:** an export or report for a *past* month uses today's policy percentages and today's role and department. Export the current month for payroll. Dated policy versions are the next step if backdated policy changes become common. |
| **Country transfers as an in-place change** | A move between countries changes currency, pay structure and payroll provider. Recording it as a leaver (*internal transfer*) plus a new hire keeps each country's history and payroll clean. |
| **Final settlement & exit processes** | Last salary, leave encashment, gratuity, notice-period recovery, exit checklists and asset return are payroll and HR-operations processes. v1 records *that* and *when* someone left and why. |
| **Deleting employees; personal-data erasure (GDPR-style)** | Pay history must survive for audits and disputes, so employees are never deleted. Lawful erasure requests need a separate, audited anonymisation process. |
| **Overtime hours & payouts** | v1 stores the overtime *rate* as policy. Recording hours worked and paying for them is timesheet/payroll work: it needs time tracking, approvals and pay periods. |
| **Per-employee allowance exceptions** | Everyone in a country + role gets the same allowances. Individual exceptions (e.g. no HRA for someone in company housing) are real but rare, and they are exactly where an audit trail and approvals matter, so they should arrive together with those later. Keeping allowances policy-based in v1 also keeps insights such as "average gross for this role" meaningful. |
| **Fixed-amount or capped allowances, statutory rules** | v1 supports allowances as a % of basic only. Fixed amounts, caps and statutory rules (e.g. Indian HRA tax exemption, PF ceilings) vary by country and law and would turn this into a payroll engine. |
| **Cross-country comparison in one currency (FX)** | Converting needs an FX-rate source and a policy (spot vs. budget rate, which date). Averaging raw numbers across currencies would be *wrong*, so v1 reports every metric per country in local currency instead of showing a misleading org-wide number. |
| **Approval workflows, bulk appraisal cycles** | Revisions are entered one employee at a time and take effect as entered. Approvals and bulk "apply 8% to this group" need a review flow that deserves its own iteration. |
| **Excel import, general exports** | Import needs de-duplication and error-reporting UX that deserves its own iteration. The demo task covers data at scale. The payroll CSV is the one export v1 needs. |
| **Variable pay payouts, performance ratings, payout schedules** | v1 stores the *target* variable pay as policy. What is actually paid depends on performance ratings, business results and payout cycles (quarterly/annual). That is a performance and payroll process with its own data and approvals. |
| **Per-employee variable pay %** | Like allowances, variable pay is set per country + role. Individual deals belong with the future audit and approval work. |
| **One-off bonuses, equity, benefits, min/max pay bands** | Total-compensation modelling multiplies data complexity. Basic + allowances + target variable pay answers the stated questions. |
| **Attrition analytics, org hierarchy / managers** | Hire and end dates make attrition reporting possible later. Neither is needed for the stated questions. |

## Non-functional
- **Tenant isolation**: no page, form, report or export can read or change another organization's data, and this is covered by automated tests.
- Handles 10,000+ employees per organization: server-side paging/filtering, indexed columns, and aggregates computed on the server, not in the browser. A country's payroll export generates in a few seconds.
- **History is preserved**: salary revisions and employment records are never deleted or rewritten, only voided or ended, so any past pay decision can be explained.
- Zero-setup local run: single command, embedded SQLite (easy to swap for Postgres).
- Built test-first: the pay calculations, business rules, pages, flows and export format are covered by automated tests.

## Assumptions
- Pay is defined **annually**, gross of tax, in whole units of local currency. Calculated allowances and target variable pay are rounded to whole units.
- **Monthly pay** = annual ÷ 12, **prorated by calendar days** employed in the month at each basic in force (joiners, leavers, mid-month revisions), and rounded to whole units per component. Country-specific proration rules (working days, 30-day months) are out of scope; calendar days is the single rule.
- **Unpaid leave** is deducted at the daily gross rate: (annual gross in force on the last day employed in the month ÷ 12) ÷ calendar days in the month × days.
- Variable pay is not part of monthly pay; its payouts are out of scope.
- Terminology: **gross pay** = basic + allowances (fixed pay); **target variable pay** = gross × the role's variable %; **total target pay** = gross + target variable pay.
- A revision takes effect from the **start** of its effective date. The last working day is the **last day employed**; the person is still counted on that day.
- "Today" and pay months use the application's configured time zone. Per-organization time zones are not in v1.
- Hourly basic pay for overtime reference = annual basic ÷ (52 weeks × the organization's standard weekly hours, default 40).
- Supported countries (v1, shared by all organizations): India, United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Canada, Australia, Singapore, Japan. Adding one is a single seed entry. Each organization picks the ones it operates in.
- Multiple HR Managers, but edits are infrequent, so conflicts are rare. Detecting a conflict and asking the user to retry (optimistic locking) is preferred over locking records while someone edits them (pessimistic locking).
